TELEMETRY PARAMETERS

Temperature: The satellite's internal/panel temperature in degrees Celsius.
Normal range is roughly 0 to 50 C, swinging with the orbit as the satellite
moves in and out of sunlight. Below -25 C or above 80 C is a critical
(high priority) anomaly, usually pointing to a thermal control system fault
or loss of attitude control exposing the wrong face to the sun.

Battery Voltage: Bus voltage in volts. Normal is about 24-36 V. A voltage
below 21 V or above 37 V is critical -- it risks damaging onboard
electronics or indicates a charging/regulation failure.

Battery Current: Current draw in amps. Normal is roughly 0.5-5 A. Very high
current (above 6.5 A) can mean a short circuit or a subsystem stuck on;
very low current with low voltage can mean a battery is failing.

Solar Panel Output: Power generated by the solar arrays, in watts. Normal
is 50-145 W depending on sun angle. A sharp, sustained drop below 30 W
outside of an expected eclipse is critical and usually means panel damage,
a deployment failure, or a pointing (attitude) problem.

Signal Strength: Downlink signal quality as a percentage. Above 40% is
considered nominal. Below 20% is critical and can mean an antenna fault,
severe attitude misalignment, or a ground-station link problem.

Fuel Level: Remaining propellant as a percentage of capacity. Below 10% is
critical -- it limits the satellite's ability to do orbit-raising or
collision-avoidance maneuvers.

Altitude and Velocity: Checked against each satellite's own nominal orbit.
Large drift in altitude or velocity suggests orbital decay, an unplanned
maneuver, or a propulsion anomaly.

Attitude Error: How far off (in degrees) the satellite's actual orientation
is from its commanded orientation. Above 5 degrees is critical and usually
cascades into other problems (temperature swings, low solar output, weak
signal) because the satellite is no longer pointed correctly.
